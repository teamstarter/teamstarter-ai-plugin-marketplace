# The marks of a generated deck: catalog and review checklist

Each entry gives the mark, why a reader takes it for generated, and the fix. "Remove" marks are
almost always a reflex; "earn it" marks are fine when they serve the audience. The organization's
template and brand guide win over this list.

## S1. Titles

- Remove: titles that name a topic (« Contexte », « Notre solution », « Résultats ») on most
  slides. Write the point of the slide as a sentence: « Les porteurs remplissent leur fiche en
  deux jours au lieu de dix ». The audience reads the deck by its titles.
- Remove: Title Case (« Notre Solution Innovante »), an emoji in a title, an exclamation mark, a
  period at the end of a short title.
- Remove: a decorative accent line under every title; a title in a different place on each slide.
- Remove: titles that are generic handles (« Architecture », « Trois piliers », « Ce que le
  budget permet »), abstract uplift (« L'IA devient une infrastructure »), tools or dates that act
  (« Votre outil vous ment », « La semaine 1 se termine par… »), and the same « Court. Court. »
  cadence on three titles or more.
- Earn it: a question as a title, once, when the slide answers a question the audience asked.

## S2. Structure of the deck

- Remove: an agenda slide in a deck of ten slides or fewer. Start with the point.
- Remove: a « À retenir » or « Conclusion » slide that repeats the deck.
- Remove: a closing « Merci ! » or « Des questions ? » slide. End on the decision asked, the next
  step, or the number to remember.
- Remove: process text left on a slide (« Option A », « à compléter », "generated from",
  "Lorem ipsum", `[Nom du client]`).
- Remove: the same layout on every slide (a title and three to five bullets). Choose the layout
  from the content: one number, a comparison, a chart, a quote, a photo of the real thing.
- Remove: a deck of text only. Show what the text describes: the screen, the chart, the table,
  the photo of the workshop.

## S3. Bullets and text

- Remove: bullets that open with a bold label and a colon (« **Rapidité :** un gain de temps »).
- Remove: emoji as bullets (✅ 🚀 💡 👉).
- Remove: three bullets by reflex, three adjectives, three icons. Use the number the content has.
- Remove: more than about 60 words of body text on a slide; a paragraph pasted on a slide. Split
  the slide or move the text to the speaker notes.
- Remove: speaker notes that repeat the slide word for word.
- The writing rules apply to every word on a slide (`ts-general-deslop-writing`): no em dash, no
  « Il ne s'agit pas de X mais de Y », no « véritable levier ».

## S4. Visuals

- Remove: icon-in-a-circle trios or quartets (« Rapidité, Simplicité, Impact ») standing for
  arguments that the slide never makes.
- Remove: « piliers », pyramids, funnels and Venn diagrams drawn without data behind them.
- Remove: stock photos of hands, handshakes, lightbulbs, and generated images where the product,
  the team or the real result should be.
- Remove: gradient backgrounds, glows, abstract 3D shapes, colored bars on the edges of slides.
- Remove: the default palette: purple or blue gradients, blue by default, a cream or beige
  background chosen by nobody. If these colors would work unchanged in another company's deck,
  nothing was decided: take the organization's template. One color carries most of the weight.
- Remove: charts with a rainbow of colors; highlight the series that makes the point, gray the
  rest.
- Earn it: an icon beside a word, when the icon set is the organization's and the icon encodes
  something.

## S5. Numbers and proof

- Remove: invented numbers (« +300 % d'engagement », « 99,9 % de satisfaction »), logos of
  customers who did not agree, testimonials without a source. Every number on a slide has its
  source and its date, in small text if needed.
- Remove: charts or gauges that measure nothing.

## S6. Layout and type

- Body text centered on every slide; the same visual weight for every element.
- Text too small to read in the room: about 14 pt for body text, 10 pt for captions at the
  least. Cut words instead of shrinking the font.
- A title, chart labels and margins that move from slide to slide.
- More than about five colors or three fonts in the deck; animation that is not progressive
  disclosure or emphasis.
- The marks of generated pages carried over to slides: a band of invented statistics, numbered
  markers 01, 02, 03 on content that is not a sequence, gradient text, a kicker above every title.

## The final question

Shown this deck and told « une IA l'a fait », would someone believe it at once? If yes, find the
slides where the layout, the title or the picture was left to the default.
